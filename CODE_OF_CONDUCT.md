# 🌱 Community Code of Conduct

Advanced CSS is a learning repository. It welcomes people who want to explore styling, browser behaviour, accessibility, and frontend fundamentals. This Code of Conduct establishes expectations for project discussions and contributions.

## 🤝 Our Commitment

We aim to maintain a respectful environment where contributors can ask questions, share examples, disagree constructively, and learn from mistakes.

## ✅ Expected Behaviour

Participants are expected to:
- Be respectful and patient.
- Welcome beginner questions.
- Critique code and ideas rather than people.
- Explain technical feedback with examples.
- Respect different backgrounds and experience levels.
- Keep discussions relevant to the project.
- Give credit where required.
- Accept corrections professionally.
- Protect private information.

## 🚫 Unacceptable Behaviour

The following behaviour is not acceptable:
- Harassment, threats, or intimidation.
- Discriminatory or hateful language.
- Personal attacks or deliberate humiliation.
- Publishing private information without permission.
- Repeated disruptive conduct.
- Sexual harassment or unwelcome advances.
- Deliberate spam or malicious participation.

## 💬 Technical Feedback

Technical disagreement is welcome. Feedback should explain the issue, impact, and possible alternative. Statements such as "this is wrong" are less useful than a specific explanation of invalid CSS, accessibility impact, or browser behaviour.

## 🧭 Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, code review, discussions, and other project-managed spaces. It may also apply to conduct outside those spaces when directly connected to project participation.

## 📣 Reporting a Concern

Contact the repository owner privately through GitHub. Include a description, links or relevant context, and any immediate safety concern. Avoid public disclosure of private information.

## ⚖️ Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove inappropriate content, issue warnings, close discussions, restrict participation, or take other reasonable action. Enforcement should be proportionate to the behaviour and its impact.

Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith is not acceptable.
